About the project

Bridging the communication gap between spoken language and sign language through anatomically grounded, real-time generative motion synthesis.

The accessibility gap in automated signing

Historically, automated sign language systems have fallen into two failure modes: concatenating pre-recorded video clips, which introduces severe visual transitions and unnatural pacing, or rigid rule-based humanoid avatars that move mechanically without organic articulation.

More critically, most prior avatars ignore non-manual features: facial expressions, eyebrow movements, and head tilt. In sign language linguistics, non-manual markers are not emotional embellishments; they are grammatical operators. Furrowed eyebrows distinguish a WH-question ("where", "who") from a statement, while a brow raise marks a yes/no question or conditional clause. Omitting them renders the output ungrammatical or incomprehensible to native Deaf signers.

Generative diffusion over continuous space

Our framework treats signing as a continuous probabilistic trajectory in 3D Euclidean space. By training a conditional diffusion denoiser over real MediaPipe Holistic human captures, the model learns the natural kinematic distribution of human articulation. Manual handshapes and non-manual facial markers are synthesized simultaneously in a single generative pass, preserving both the sign vocabulary and its grammatical context.

Ethics and Deaf-centered evaluation

The sign language processing research community has historically caused harm by deploying systems without the consultation, leadership, or validation of the Deaf and hard-of-hearing community.

To ensure clinical credibility and authentic utility, our evaluation protocol separates technical metrics (such as Average Position Error and jerk ratio) from human intelligibility testing. Intelligibility trials are conducted with certified sign language interpreters and Deaf signers using a pre-reveal free-recall protocol: participants record what they understood before the target text is revealed, preventing priming contamination.
