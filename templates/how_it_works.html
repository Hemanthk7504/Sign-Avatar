How the synthesis pipeline works

A low-latency, modular pipeline converting spoken audio or free-form text into continuous, grammatically accurate 3D signing motion.

01

Audio ingestion and speech recognition

Microphone input is captured natively in the browser via the Web Audio API as 16kHz uncompressed linear PCM WAV buffers. This eliminates external transcoding dependencies like FFmpeg on the server and feeds directly into local or streaming speech-to-text models.

Latency budget: < 150ms per audio chunk. Direct PCM pass-through prevents codec compression lag.
02

Linguistic analysis and non-manual marker tagging

English text is parsed to approximate sign-language grammar: stopwords and copulas are dropped, time words are fronted, and intent is classified. Questions and negations are tagged with grammatical non-manual markers (such as raised eyebrows for yes/no questions, furrowed brows for WH-questions, and head shakes for negation).

Linguistic preservation: In sign language, non-manual signals are grammatical requirements, not optional emotional expressions.
03

Diffusion-based motion synthesis

A conditional 4-layer Transformer Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) operates on an anatomically informed 66-point body representation (8 upper-body points, 21 joints per hand, and 16 facial landmarks). Sampling is accelerated using DDIM respacing across 25 steps, synthesizing complete signs 3 to 5 times faster than real-time on CPU.

Coarticulation: Neighbouring signs are cross-faded using cosine blend transitions, ensuring smooth hand travel without teleportation.
04

Real-time WebGL rendering and streaming

Motion frames stream over WebSockets in low-latency chunks as each sign is synthesized. In the browser, Three.js updates vertex position buffers at 25+ FPS, driven by a clock-synced animation loop that plays smoothly even under background tab throttling.

GPU footprint: Zero client-side neural inference; the browser only executes lightweight WebGL line/point updates.
